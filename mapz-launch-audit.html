MapZ Launch Audit

MapZ is a web GIS platform for real-estate developers. It turns plotted layouts, Google Sheets inventory, documents and 360° photos into a shareable interactive map, and answers plain-English questions with AI. This audit lists everything that has to be fixed, bought, signed or set up before it can run as a paying business in India.

Method: passive review of the public site, its JavaScript bundle and its public API responses Not tested: logged-in app, database rules, server code

Verdict: not ready to charge customers yet

The product is far along. The front end is polished and the feature set is clear. The gaps are in the business layer: licences, legal paperwork, payments, and a few data-exposure issues that could hurt the first developer who trusts it with buyer records. Fix these four first:

  1. Satellite imagery is pulled straight from Google's tile servers with no API key or licence.
  2. The public demo map is set to show buyer names, photos and sale deeds to anyone with the link.
  3. The public map API hands every visitor the developer's Google Sheet IDs, so raw sheets may be readable.
  4. There is no Terms of Service page and no named legal entity, and the contact address is a Gmail account.

Launch blockers

  • FoundThe map fetches tiles directly from mt1.google.com/vt/lyrs=s… with no API key. The public demo map uses Google Satellite as its default basemap. Google only licenses its imagery through Maps Platform APIs with billing turned on. Google can block the requests without warning, which would leave every customer's public map blank, and the business carries the licence risk.

    FixMove satellite and street layers to a licensed source: Google's Map Tiles API (2D tiles, API key, metered with a free monthly allowance), Esri World Imagery through an ArcGIS Location Platform account, MapTiler Satellite or Mapbox Satellite. Build the provider's per-map-view cost into plan pricing.

  • FoundThe public demo at /public/map/public (340 views) is configured to show these fields to anyone: Vendee_Name, Person_s_Photo, Sale_Deed_Attachment, Document_no, Patta_No, Date_of_Registration. I did not open the records. If they belong to real buyers, this publishes their names, photos and title documents without their consent. It is also the first thing a prospective developer sees.

    FixSwap the demo to made-up data today. In the product, make personal fields private by default. Show a warning when a developer publishes a column whose name looks like a name, phone, photo, Aadhaar, PAN or deed.

  • FoundGET /api/public/map/{token} returns each layer's source_config.spreadsheetId to anonymous visitors. Many developers share their sheets as "anyone with the link". Anyone holding the ID can then open the whole raw sheet, every column and not just the fields chosen for the map. The homepage promises that view-only sharing protects raw spreadsheets, and this undoes that promise.

    FixKeep source IDs, sheet names and table IDs on the server. The server should fetch the data, drop every column not on the map's allowed list, and return only those fields. Cache the result for a short time.

  • FoundThe footer links to Terms of Service, but /terms is a 404. The privacy policy says the registered entity and address are "subject to final corporate confirmation". The public contact is a Gmail address. Payment gateways, Google's OAuth review and enterprise buyers all check for these.

    FixIncorporate (see the legal section), then publish Terms of Service, a full Privacy Policy, Refund and Cancellation policy, About, Contact and Pricing pages under the company's name. Move all contact to @mapz.in addresses.

Legal and compliance (India)

This is a checklist to take to a lawyer and a CA, not legal advice. Items marked "confirm" depend on how they read your specific setup.

  • Private Limited company (better if you plan to raise money) or LLP. Then PAN, TAN and a current bank account. GST registration becomes compulsory once turnover passes ₹20 lakh, but register early anyway: developers want GST invoices to claim input credit. Also Karnataka Shops & Establishments registration and Professional Tax. Udyam (MSME) and DPIIT startup recognition are optional, and DPIIT can bring tax benefits.

  • "MapZ" is short and common in mapping. Run a search in Classes 9 and 42 on the IP India portal before spending on the brand, then file. The code uses both mapz.in and mapz.online. Pick one canonical domain and redirect the other.

  • The Rules were notified in November 2025. Most duties (notice, consent, security, breach reporting, deletion) apply from about May 2027, but enterprise customers will ask about them now. There are two roles. For buyer data inside a developer's map, the developer is the Data Fiduciary and MapZ is their Data Processor, so every customer contract needs a Data Processing Agreement. For MapZ's own users and enquiry leads, MapZ is the Fiduciary and must give notice, take consent, publish a grievance contact, report breaches, and delete data on request. Penalties for failing to protect data go up to ₹250 crore.

  • FoundThe current policy only covers people who submit an enquiry.

    AddAccount holders and the Google data you access, including Google's required "Limited Use" disclosure. Gemini processing of questions. Every sub-processor and where it stores data (Supabase, Vercel, Brevo, Google). Cookies from the YouTube embed. Browser voice input, which sends audio to Google in Chrome. Retention periods for maps and uploaded files. A named grievance officer.

  • MapZ hosts content its customers upload (documents, photos), so the IT Rules 2021 apply. Publish a grievance officer's name and contact. Acknowledge complaints within 24 hours and resolve them within 15 days. Ban unlawful content in the Terms. CERT-In's 2022 directions require reporting cyber incidents within 6 hours and keeping system logs for 180 days in India.

  • India's 2021 geospatial guidelines (kept in the 2022 National Geospatial Policy) say map data more accurate than 1 m horizontal or 3 m vertical must be owned by an Indian entity and stored and processed in India. Plot surveys and drone orthomosaics are usually centimetre-level. The API call I traced ran through Vercel's US region.

    FixPut the Supabase project and storage in Mumbai (ap-south-1) and pin Vercel functions to bom1. Ask counsel whether customer GeoTIFFs and survey layers count as finer-than-threshold data.

  • A public project map with prices and availability is marketing. Under Section 11(2) of the RERA Act, promoter ads must show the project's registration number and the authority's website. Maharashtra, Kerala, UP and Tamil Nadu also require a QR code, and MahaRERA has penalised hundreds of projects for leaving it out. Karnataka has reportedly done the same, so confirm with K-RERA.

    FixMake the RERA number and QR code a required field in the public map header. The Terms should make the developer responsible for ad compliance.

  • Buyers will rely on plot dimensions, facing and survey numbers. If a plot turns out smaller than shown, the buyer may blame the platform. Put a short notice on every public map saying it is indicative and not a survey or title document. The Terms should require the developer to confirm their data is accurate and that they hold rights to it. Cap MapZ's liability at 12 months of fees and get an indemnity from customers for their content.

  • Click-through Terms of Service for self-serve plans. A Master Subscription Agreement and Order Form for larger deals. A Data Processing Agreement. An Acceptable Use Policy. A Refund and Cancellation policy. An SLA, but only if you promise uptime. A cookie notice.

  • If MapZ flies drones for clients, the Drone Rules 2021 apply: a registered drone (UIN), a certified remote pilot, and Digital Sky permission in yellow and red zones. If customers upload their own imagery, the Terms should put rights and permits on them. Blur faces and number plates in 360° shots used on public maps.

  • Leaflet, MapLibre, Pannellum, JSZip and the other bundled libraries are permissively licensed, but they need a licences page. OpenStreetMap data needs visible "© OpenStreetMap contributors" credit, and every imagery provider has its own attribution rule.

  • Cyber liability plus professional indemnity (errors and omissions) cover. Enterprise developers often ask for both before signing.

APIs, services and plugins

  • There is no checkout. Use Razorpay Subscriptions or Cashfree. Razorpay checks the website for About, Contact, Pricing, Terms, Privacy and Refund pages and asks for a sample invoice before it activates the account. Card auto-debit above ₹15,000 needs the customer to approve each charge under RBI's e-mandate rules. Sell larger annual plans on invoice, paid by bank transfer or UPI AutoPay. Generate GST invoices automatically with Zoho Books or Razorpay Invoices.

  • Reading customers' Sheets and Drive needs Google to verify the app. Without it, users see an "unverified app" warning and there is a cap on users. Request drive.file through the Picker (already loaded on the site) instead of full Drive access. Full Drive is a "restricted" scope that needs a paid CASA security assessment every year. The Sheets scope is "sensitive": you need a verified domain, the full privacy policy and a short demo video.

  • On Gemini's free tier, Google may use prompts to improve its products. Customer project data must not go there. Link billing to the Google Cloud project or use Vertex AI so paid-tier data terms apply. Set a budget alert and a hard monthly cap.

  • Vercel's Hobby plan forbids commercial use, so confirm the project is on Pro and set the function region to Mumbai. Supabase's free tier pauses idle projects and has no backups. Use Pro with point-in-time recovery in the Mumbai region. I couldn't see which plans are in use now.

  • OpenStreetMap's own tile servers run on donated capacity. Their usage policy limits heavy and app-scale use and lets them block traffic. The default basemap points straight at them. Switch to MapTiler or Stadia Maps, or host your own Protomaps file on cheap storage. Check CARTO's terms before using its free basemaps commercially. I couldn't confirm them.

  • Authenticate mapz.in in Brevo with SPF, DKIM and a DMARC policy so lead alerts don't land in spam. Send from notifications@mapz.in, which the privacy policy already promises. Keep any marketing email on a separate subdomain.

  • Add Cloudflare Turnstile (free) to the enquiry form and to Ask MapZ on public maps. A bot posting fake leads, or running up AI calls on the account, is the most likely early abuse.

  • Many developers' staff use Microsoft 365 or plain email. Add email magic links (built into Supabase Auth) and Microsoft sign-in. Require MFA for the super-admin account.

  • Sentry for front-end and API errors. An uptime check on the public map and API. Vercel log drains kept 180 days for CERT-In. Product analytics such as PostHog, only after cookie consent.

  • A help desk (Zoho Desk or Freshdesk), a help centre with import guides, a status page, and the WhatsApp Business API once chat volume grows past one phone.

  • Switch the YouTube embed to youtube-nocookie.com. Self-host Pannellum, or load it with a pinned integrity hash, instead of pulling it live from jsDelivr.

Security

"Found" means seen on the live site. "Verify" means it needs the source code or database to confirm, and I didn't probe those.

  • FoundPublic, logged-out map pages call POST /api/proxy/sheets (and there are matching teable, nocodb and drive routes) with a JSON body chosen by the browser.

    FixMake sure each request is tied to a valid share token, is limited to that map's layers and allowed fields, and can never use a customer's stored Google, Teable or NocoDB credentials to fetch something else. If not, anyone can use the server to read other customers' data.

  • FoundWhich features each plan unlocks (Ask MapZ, linked data, enquiries, reports, white-label) is worked out in browser code.

    FixEvery paid API must check the organisation's plan on the server. Otherwise a free user can edit one variable and get Plus.

  • The Supabase public key in the bundle is normal, but it is only safe if row-level security is on for every table and storage bucket and each policy limits rows to the user's organisation. Run Supabase's Security Advisor and fix everything it flags.

  • FoundOnly HSTS is set. There is no Content-Security-Policy, framing rule, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy or Permissions-Policy. The homepage also sends Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *.

    FixAdd them in vercel.json. Allow framing only on /public/map/*, since public maps are meant to be embedded, and block it everywhere else to prevent clickjacking on the admin. Make sure API routes never return a wildcard CORS header on requests that carry credentials.

  • Google refresh tokens and customers' Teable and NocoDB API tokens should be encrypted at rest (Supabase Vault), never sent to the browser, and revoked when a customer disconnects. The disconnect endpoint already exists.

  • Uploads go up to 25 MB and include ZIP, KML, GPX, GeoTIFF and PDF. Check the real file type on the server, cap unzipped size to stop zip bombs, and malware-scan PDFs. Serve user files from a separate domain or as downloads so an uploaded file can't run scripts on mapz.in.

  • The demo uses the slug public, and custom slugs are a selling point. That's fine for maps meant to be public. Unlisted maps should get long random tokens, with an optional password and an expiry date.

  • The enquiry form is rate-limited, according to the privacy policy. Apply the same to login, Ask MapZ, the proxy routes and the public map API.

  • /api/auth/super-admin-google should accept a fixed list of accounts only, require MFA, and log every action. An edit-history API exists, which is a good start.

  • Large developers and any government work will ask for a VAPT report from a CERT-In empanelled auditor. Do a restore drill from backup. Write a one-page incident plan that covers the 6-hour CERT-In report and DPDP breach notices.

Ask MapZ: AI guardrails and token cost

The safe, cheap shape for this feature

Question→Rule-based fast path→Cache→Gemini returns a filter as JSON→Server validates it→App runs the filter→Map highlights plots

The model only translates words into a filter like Status = VACANT AND Phase = 2. It never sees the rows, never writes SQL and never invents the answer. The count on screen comes from your own code.

  • Give the model field names, types and a short list of each field's real values (Status: VACANT, BOOKED, SOLD). Leave out every column not on the map's allowed list. This keeps buyer names and documents out of the AI provider entirely, and it is also the largest token saving.

  • Use Gemini's response schema so it must return JSON. Reject any field not in the layer, any operator not on your list, and any value of the wrong type. When it fails, say "I couldn't turn that into a filter" and show example questions.

  • A cell can contain "ignore your instructions…". Sample values go into the prompt as quoted data, never into the system instructions. Questions that aren't about the map get a fixed reply with no free-form chat.

  • Set a daily cap per organisation and per visitor IP, and a 300-character question limit. Use a short timeout and log usage per organisation so it can be billed. Keep a switch that turns AI off for one map or for everyone. If anonymous visitors on public maps can use Ask MapZ, that is your largest cost exposure, so put Turnstile in front of it.

  • "Available plots", "sold", "phase 2", "east facing" and "corner plots" can be matched with keyword rules against the layer's real values. Most questions on a plotted layout are like this, and these cost nothing.

  • Cache results by normalised question plus a hash of the layer's schema. Put instructions and schema first and the question last, so Gemini's context caching reuses the shared prefix. Shorten long column names like North_to_South_on_Eastern_side_in_feet_and_inches to short aliases in the prompt. Drop geometry, photo and attachment fields.

  • Turning text into a filter is a small task. Use the cheapest current Gemini Flash-Lite model at temperature 0 with a 150-token output cap. Handle distance questions ("within 500 m", "faces the park") with real geometry code such as Turf.js or PostGIS. The model only names which operation to run.

  • Show the applied filter as removable chips so people can see what was searched. Note that answers aren't legal or valuation advice. Voice input uses the browser's speech service, which sends audio to Google in Chrome and doesn't work in Firefox. Say so next to the mic button.

Business plumbing still missing

  • The Essential and Plus plans and the ₹5,000 report add-on exist only inside the app's code. The site's only call to action is "Contact Us". Publish prices, or "from ₹X per project", and offer a free trial on one sample project.

  • Upgrades, downgrades, renewal reminders, retrying failed payments, grace periods, and what happens to public maps when a plan lapses. Decide all of this before the first renewal date, not after.

  • Let customers export their layers (GeoJSON, CSV) and media whenever they like. Delete everything within a set time after they cancel. DPDP requires the deletion, and enterprise buyers ask about both.

  • /sitemap.xml returns the app page instead of a sitemap. The social preview image uses a relative path, so WhatsApp and LinkedIn previews may not show it. Use a full https:// URL. Every public project map is also a page that could rank for "plots in [area]" with the developer's permission.

  • A sample project, a sheet template with the expected columns and geometry format, and a 2-minute setup video. Converting CAD layouts to polygons will be the hardest step for most developers. Consider doing it for them as a paid service.

What this audit could not see

This was a look from the outside only. To finish the security items marked "Verify", I'd need the repository and read access to the Supabase project. That would cover row-level security policies, server-side plan checks, proxy authorisation, token storage, upload handling and the logged-in app.
